YOUR INFORMATION

Privacy Policy for KeyPress

Effective date: 19 October 2026

KeyPress is a desktop application for creating, recording, and running mouse and keyboard automations. This policy explains the information the application handles, where it is handled, and when it may leave your device.

Summary

KeyPress is designed to work locally. Trying it does not require an account, and it does not include advertising, analytics, or telemetry. Your macros, recordings, settings, and routine automation activity stay on your device unless you choose to use an Anthropic-powered feature, choose to send a support report, or configure a macro to save or share data through another application or service.

KeyPress has a free plan with up to three saved macros. A one-off licence or subscription adds unlimited macros. Buying either, or starting the free AI trial, creates an account with the KeyPress service when you complete the checkout; nothing else does. The Anthropic-powered features in the version of KeyPress sold to the public are provided through that service, which passes requests on to Anthropic and keeps a small record of your purchase and AI credit. (Builds made by developers from the source code can instead use their own Anthropic API key, in which case requests go from the computer straight to Anthropic.) The sections below say exactly what each path sends and keeps.

Information KeyPress Handles

Depending on the features you use, KeyPress may handle:

  • Macro content, including macro names, workflow steps, typed text, coordinates, hotkeys, screen regions, conditions, and values you enter into a macro.
  • Keyboard and mouse input while you deliberately use the recording feature. Recording is active only while you start it; its pause, checkpoint, and stop controls are intended to let you control what is captured.
  • Clipboard contents when you add and run a macro step that reads, writes, or pastes clipboard data.
  • Files on your device that you point a macro at. A Read File step reads the text file you name (up to 10 MB) into the macro so later steps can use it, and a Write File step adds text to, or replaces the contents of, the file you name. Neither step searches for files, and both work only on the full path you configure.
  • Screen content when you use screen selection, image, OCR, screenshot, or AI features. Screen captures can contain personal, confidential, or third-party information visible on your screen.
  • Secrets you add under Settings, such as a password a macro types. Each value is encrypted for your Windows user account and stored in a local file; the macro itself stores only the secret's name. No value is ever shown back, logged, written to a run trace, or sent to the assistant. A run that records screens does not read the words of a step that typed a secret, but it still photographs the screen, so a secret typed into a field that shows its characters can appear in those pictures. A watched demonstration holds to the same rule for a password typed into a field Windows identifies as a password box: no picture, no reading of the screen and no characters are kept while it is typed, and the screen that follows is not described.
  • Recorded run screens, only if you turn on "Record screens" for a run: a reduced picture of your whole screen before each step that touches the machine, a full-resolution crop around each step's target, and the words KeyPress reads in the areas that changed. This is off by default, applies to that one run only, and is never started by a hotkey, the tray or a held trigger.
  • A watched demonstration, only when you ask Claude in the Assistant to watch you and then choose how it starts on the card it shows first. Nothing is recorded until you choose. The card has two separate choices, each made fresh every time, neither on by default and neither something Claude can turn on. "Start recording" records your clicks and typing as the ordinary recorder does, plus any notes you type to Claude while it records. "Start, and let Claude see my screen" also keeps, while you show the task, a reduced picture of your whole screen at each step you take (a click, a run of typing, a shortcut, a burst of scrolling), reads the words in the areas that changed, and names the control under each click. The microphone tick turns your speech into text with Windows' own speech recogniser, on your computer: the audio is not stored or sent, only the recognised words are kept, as notes on the step you are on, and they go to the Assistant like typed notes. Nothing is captured or heard while the recording is paused, what you do inside KeyPress itself is left out, and nothing is captured, read or kept about a password typed into a field Windows identifies as a password box.
  • Window, process, and accessibility information when a macro uses corresponding automation steps.
  • Preferences such as the last macro opened, recording controls, kill-switch shortcut, startup setting, and the timestamp/version of your consent to AI computer-use mode.
  • An Anthropic API key if you choose to configure one in the application. On Windows, a key saved through KeyPress is encrypted using Windows’ user-scoped data protection. You may alternatively provide it through the ANTHROPIC_API_KEY environment variable; KeyPress does not write that environment variable.
  • A licence token, once you buy a licence or subscription or start the free AI trial. It is the credential that identifies your account to the KeyPress service. It is stored on your device in the same encrypted way as an API key, and is never shown or exported.
  • A licence certificate: a small record, signed by the KeyPress service, saying which plan your account holds and until when, so KeyPress can check it without going online. It contains your account's identifier (a random code, not your name or email), the plan, whether it includes AI, whether you own the one-off licence, and its dates. It is stored encrypted on your device and refreshed from the service about once a day.
  • The date your free trial started, stored encrypted on your device so KeyPress can count the 30 days.

Local Storage

KeyPress stores saved macros and groups as JSON files in a Keypress folder inside your Documents folder, so that uninstalling the application does not delete them. These files may contain any information you put into a macro, including text, workflow values, and automation instructions. Documents may be backed up or synchronized by Windows, OneDrive or another service you configured; KeyPress does not control those copies.

Preferences, saved credentials, chats, logs, support outbox files and temporary screen data live in the application's data and configuration directories. The standalone Windows uninstaller removes these managed data locations for the Windows account running the uninstaller, including its WebView browser data. Close KeyPress first; if cleanup fails, the uninstaller reports the failure so you can retry. Data belonging to other Windows accounts is left alone. If you set XDG_DATA_HOME or XDG_CONFIG_HOME, use the same environment when uninstalling or remove the custom locations yourself. A custom XDG_DATA_HOME also holds the macro library; uninstall preserves its macro and group files. Uninstalling does not cancel a subscription, erase the service's account records, or remove copies in backups or other applications.

During recording, target picking or AI authoring, KeyPress may create local image patches used to help identify screen locations and visual targets, and wider pictures of the area around each target that a macro step shows on its card. These images can contain portions of your screen. Discarding a recording removes its unused images. Permanently deleting a saved macro also removes the app-managed recording images that file still references, except images referenced by another saved macro or group. Save any copies you want to keep before deleting the original. Cleanup refuses linked recording folders and stops if other saved files cannot be checked safely. Removing an individual step keeps its images for undo; finished but unsaved recordings, removed-step images, and images retained by saved groups can remain until you discard them or remove the images or application data yourself. Images you supplied from elsewhere and screenshot files you configured a macro to write are not deleted with a macro. Deleting a macro does not delete its text from saved Assistant chats; delete those separately in History.

The pictures of a watched demonstration are written to the same runs folder, are deleted as soon as the recording stops, and are never served to anything. Only a short text summary and at most three small screenshots (see below) are kept for the Assistant conversation: the screenshots in memory only, the summary in the conversation, which is saved with the chat.

KeyPress keeps a diagnostic log of its own activity in a logs folder in the application data directory: the current session's log and the previous session's, plus a record of the previous session's crash if there was one. The log records what KeyPress did - which step ran, how long it took, whether it succeeded - and is written so that it does not contain the text of your macro steps, values a macro reads from the screen, clipboard or files, window titles, or file paths. It does name your macros, for example when a hotkey starts one; when a log is attached to a support report, those names are masked too. Older logs are replaced automatically. The log never leaves your device unless you attach it to a support report you choose to send.

Recorded run screens, when you turn them on, are stored in a runs folder in the application data directory. They are kept only for the most recent few runs, are deleted when a run falls out of that window, and the whole folder is cleared each time KeyPress starts. They are served only to KeyPress's own window and never leave your device.

Assistant chats - what you typed, Claude's replies, and the text of the macro sent with them - are saved in an assistant-chats folder in the application data directory so you can reopen them from the Assistant's History list. Only the 50 most recent are kept, each can be deleted from that list, and they never leave your device except as part of a message you send to Claude. A chat file holds the text of what Claude was told, which for a watched demonstration includes the summary of the steps, the words read from your screen and the notes you typed or spoke; it never holds a screenshot, which is replaced by a note.

A Write File step creates or changes only the file named in the step, and does not create folders. Replacing a file cannot be undone by KeyPress.

KeyPress may also create a local screenshot if you explicitly configure a screenshot action to write one to a file. The location and retention of that file are determined by the macro configuration and your device’s file system.

When Information Leaves Your Device

KeyPress does not send routine automation, macro, recording, clipboard, or screen data to KeyPress’s developer. The exceptions are the optional subscription described below, whose requests pass through a service the developer runs on their way to Anthropic, and a support report, which is sent only when you review it and press Send (see "Support Reports You Choose to Send").

Information is sent to Anthropic only if you choose to configure and run an Anthropic-powered feature:

  • Ask Claude macro steps: KeyPress sends the question you configure and, if you choose a screen or region view, the corresponding screenshot. The screenshot is captured in memory for that request rather than first being saved as a temporary screenshot file.
  • The Assistant chat: each message you send goes to Anthropic with the conversation so far, a description of the macro on the canvas (its steps and their settings), and a catalog of KeyPress's step types. A screenshot is sent only when you attach one, when you ask Claude to repair a failed run that kept its screens (the frame before the failing step), when you ask Claude to drive the screen (below), or when you choose to let Claude see your screen while it watches you show a task.
  • The Assistant chat, when it watches you. If you ask Claude to watch you, it first shows a card asking how to start, and nothing is recorded until you choose. Claude is then sent the steps your clicks and typing became, as it is for any recording, and any notes you type to it while you record. If you tick "Also narrate with my microphone", the words Windows' own speech recogniser makes of what you say are added as notes on the step you are on; the audio is recognised on your computer and is never stored or sent, and the recogniser is stopped whenever the recording ends (and ends by itself if KeyPress quits or crashes). If you choose "Start, and let Claude see my screen", KeyPress also sends a text summary of the demonstration, one entry per step (what you did, the window, the control that was clicked, and the words that appeared or changed on screen afterwards, read by character recognition), and at most three reduced screenshots. The summary is bounded to a small fraction of a conversation, but it can contain whatever your screen showed, so use the pause key before anything private.
  • What a watched demonstration leaves out. Nothing is captured, read or sent while the recording is paused, or about what changed on screen across a pause. What you do inside KeyPress itself (typing a note to Claude, pressing Stop) is left out of a watched demonstration. Text typed into a field that Windows identifies as a password box is not recorded, is never in the summary, and nothing on screen is captured, read or sent while it is typed (the closing screenshot is also skipped when it was the last thing you did); a field that shows its characters and does not identify itself as a password box cannot be told apart, which is why the pause key exists.
  • What a watched demonstration leaves behind. The screenshots are kept in memory in the conversation, not saved in the chat history, and all but the newest two are replaced by a note when you send the next message. The text summary is part of the conversation and is saved with the chat (see Local Storage).
  • AI authoring / computer-use mode: After you provide the required consent, KeyPress may send your stated task goal, screen captures of the active target window (or, where necessary, the desktop), and related context to Anthropic so Claude can propose or perform authoring actions. This mode can also control your mouse and keyboard while it is active.

With your own key, these requests go directly from your computer to Anthropic and are made using your Anthropic API key. Anthropic’s handling of information sent to its services is governed by the terms and privacy documentation applicable to your Anthropic account and API use. With the KeyPress subscription they are made through the KeyPress service instead, as described in the next section, and Anthropic’s handling is governed by the terms and privacy documentation applicable to the publisher’s Anthropic account. Review Anthropic’s materials before using these features, particularly when your screen may contain sensitive information.

The KeyPress Service: Licences, Subscriptions and AI

When you buy the one-off licence or a subscription, or start the free AI trial, KeyPress opens a checkout page in your browser; when the checkout completes, KeyPress stores a licence token on your device. The one-off licence does not include the AI features. With a subscription or the AI trial, each Anthropic-powered request - including the question you configure, any screenshot sent with it, and your task goal and screen captures in AI authoring - is sent to the KeyPress service at ai.keypress.download, which runs on Cloudflare. The service checks your licence and credit, passes the request on to Anthropic using the publisher’s own API key, and returns the answer.

What the KeyPress service does not keep. It holds the content of a request in memory only for as long as it takes to check it and pass it on. It does not store prompts, screenshots, or answers, and its own logs do not contain them. Those logs do record, for each request, the opaque identifier for your subscription described below, whether the request succeeded, and what it cost.

What the KeyPress service keeps. For each subscription: the payment provider’s customer and subscription identifiers, the plan, whether it is active, the billing period, how much of the AI credit has been used (as a running amount, not a record of prompts), and when the account was created. For a one-off licence: the payment provider’s payment reference and the date it was paid, so a refund can be matched to it. For purchases and trials, the service also keeps a keyed one-way hash of the checkout email address linked to the purchase account, so you can restore your purchase later. This lookup is retained while the account is kept, including after a subscription expires or a payment is refunded, and is removed when the account is erased. It does not store the readable address, but it can match the same address again using its private hashing key. A separate keyed email hash prevents a second free trial for the same address. Your licence token is stored only as a one-way hash, so the service cannot recover it, and the sign-in code used to link your payment to your computer is stored the same way and expires within half an hour. The service also keeps payment-revocation and subscription-end facts, paid-period references and processed webhook identifiers to prevent duplicate or stale payment events from restoring access. Daily total spending and request reservations enforce the AI budget; these contain amounts and account references, not prompts or screenshots. The service does not store your name, readable email address, or card details.

Service housekeeping. A daily cleanup removes aggregate spending totals older than about 30 days, processed payment-event identifiers older than about 90 days, and stale request reservations. Refund, subscription-end and trial-prevention facts have no automatic expiry, because they stop an old or duplicate payment event from granting access again. Account-erasure requests include a review of these linked records; sign-out and uninstall do not erase them.

Restoring a purchase. To sign in on another computer, after reinstalling, or after buying on the website without KeyPress open, you can type the email address you bought with into KeyPress, which sends it to the KeyPress service. The service hashes the address and looks for a matching purchase account in its own saved lookup. If one is found, it emails a six-digit code to the address you entered; typing the code into the same copy of KeyPress signs it in. The email is sent through Cloudflare's email service, which therefore handles the address and the message under Cloudflare's terms. KeyPress gives the same answer whether or not the address bought anything, and emails nothing to an address that did not. The readable address is processed for that request and email, not saved in the database. For each request the service keeps, for about a day, a keyed one-way hash of the address (used to limit how many codes one address can be sent - three an hour), a keyed one-way hash of the code, which purchase account it was for, and when it was asked for and used. Those temporary records are separate from the longer-lived purchase lookup above. The code works once, for ten minutes, and stops working after five wrong attempts. Your IP address is used, without being kept, to limit how fast restores can be attempted.

What Cloudflare handles. The service runs on Cloudflare, so each request reaches it through Cloudflare’s network, which necessarily sees your IP address in order to deliver it, and Cloudflare keeps its own traffic statistics under its own terms. The service itself does not record your IP address, location, or device details. It records a short line for each request instead - the opaque identifier for your subscription, whether the request succeeded, and what it cost - which the publisher uses to keep the service working and to deal with abuse. Your IP address is also used, without being kept, to limit how fast sign-ins can be attempted.

What Anthropic receives. The request itself, plus an opaque identifier for your subscription, which is a one-way hash that does not reveal who you are, so that Anthropic can tell requests from different subscribers apart. Anthropic may retain and use what it receives as described in its own terms and privacy documentation for the publisher’s account.

Payment. Payment is handled by Stripe, which acts as the merchant of record for the licence and the subscriptions and handles the tax and payment terms shown at checkout. The free AI trial asks for an email address and no card. Stripe collects your payment details, email address, and billing country, under Stripe’s privacy policy. The publisher does not receive your card details, and can see in Stripe’s dashboard the customer information Stripe shows its merchants, such as your email address and country.

Limits and shutting it off. The service checks your sign-in, subscription, request shape, remaining allowance, concurrent requests and the service's daily budget before sending any request content to Anthropic. It bounds the possible cost locally; it does not send your content to an external token-counting endpoint first. A request refused by those checks is not passed to Anthropic. Once an accepted request has been sent, a provider failure, timeout or later accounting failure can still result in an error after Anthropic received it. The publisher can switch AI off for everyone at once.

Two further steps, listed under "Developer tools" in the step palette, can send information elsewhere, and only if you add and run them:

  • Web Request macro steps: KeyPress sends the HTTP or HTTPS request you configure - the address, any headers and the body you typed - to the server at that address, and reads the reply. Header values (which may hold a token) are saved in the macro file in plain text; do not share a macro that holds one. KeyPress excludes header values from its logs and run reports, and replaces them with a private-value marker in the Assistant's automatic macro description, detailed tools, repair context and edit results. The Assistant can retain an existing masked header while editing other settings. Anything you type directly into a chat, script, URL, request body or another ordinary text field is still part of the context you choose to send; use the dedicated header fields for header credentials. An address can also be supplied by an earlier step, so check where a macro you did not write sends data.
  • Run Script macro steps: KeyPress runs the PowerShell script you wrote on your computer, as you, with whatever access you have. A script can read files, use the network and change settings; what it sends or does is outside KeyPress's control. Scripts only run when the macro runs - never in a dry run unless that step is armed - and the Check button lists every script step in a macro. Read the script in any macro someone else made before running it.

Outside the Microsoft Store (an installation from the Store is updated by the Store and makes no such request), once a day at most while it is running, KeyPress fetches https://keypress.download/latest.json to find out whether a newer version exists; this is a normal web request that sends nothing but what any such request carries (such as your IP address, which the website host can see) - no identifier, no version, no macro or usage data - and you can turn it off by setting the environment variable KEYPRESS_NO_UPDATE_CHECK to 1.

KeyPress does not sell personal information or use it for targeted advertising.

Support Reports You Choose to Send

KeyPress can prepare a report to send to the publisher when something goes wrong, or when you want to request a feature or ask a question. Nothing is ever sent automatically: KeyPress shows you every item in the report, exactly as it will be sent, and sends it only when you press Send. You can save the same report as a file instead and email it yourself.

What a report can contain. What you write in the report form; the type of report; the KeyPress version and basic facts about your computer (Windows version, language, display count and scaling, which text-recognition engine is installed); for a problem report, the structure of the macro involved and what KeyPress recorded about the run that failed - which steps ran, in what order, which output each took, how long each took, and the error; the problems the Check button would report; and, if you leave them switched on, the KeyPress logs described above. You can switch off the computer facts and the logs before sending. A request, question or feedback report carries no macro, no run and no logs.

What a report never contains. For automatically collected diagnostics and report text, before anything is shown to you, KeyPress removes: the text you typed into the macro (including passwords, messages, prompts, web addresses and scripts), anything a macro read while running (screen text, clipboard contents, file contents, answers from Claude or from a person), the names of on-screen controls, window titles, file and folder paths, your Windows user name and computer name, and the values of saved secrets. A removed value is replaced by a marker that says only how long it was. A report never automatically collects screenshots, macro pictures, recorded run screens or your Assistant chats. Images you explicitly attach are the exception described below; their pixels are not redacted.

Where it goes. A report is sent to the KeyPress support service at support.keypress.download, which runs on Cloudflare. The service stores the report file in Cloudflare storage, where only the publisher can read it, and adds an entry to a work list in the publisher's private GitHub repository so the publisher can track it. That entry holds only these facts about the report: its type and identifier, the KeyPress version and release channel, which kind of step failed and how the run ended, whether a Fix with Claude repair was tried, a code that groups reports of the same problem, and the names and sizes of the files inside the report. It never holds anything you wrote or the contents of the report file. The email address you give, if any, is kept only by the support service and is used only to email you about that report: to confirm it arrived, to send you the publisher's reply, and to tell you when a version that fixes it is out (that last email may also ask whether you would review KeyPress). Those emails are sent through Cloudflare's email service from mail.keypress.download, and contain only what the publisher wrote, never what you wrote. If you reply to one, your reply reaches the publisher's own mailbox like any other email you send them. Cloudflare necessarily sees your IP address in order to deliver the report, and the service uses it, without keeping it, to limit how many reports can be sent at once.

Optional image attachments. You can attach up to five PNG or JPEG images to any report, request, question or feedback. Only images you select are included; KeyPress does not capture your screen for a report. Attached images are sent as shown, without redacting their pixels, so remove private information first. KeyPress removes image metadata and original filenames, lets you review and remove attachments, and includes them in the same report file with the same retention and deletion controls.

Replies, retention and deletion. Each report has an identifier and a private code kept on your device; KeyPress uses them to show the report's status and the publisher's reply under My reports, and asks for that status only when you open that page. A report file is deleted 90 days after its problem is marked fixed or closed, and otherwise 180 days after it was sent; at that point the service deletes the report file, your email address and the GitHub entry's facts, and keeps only the report's identifier, type and status. You can delete a report and everything stored with it at any time from My reports, or by emailing its identifier to the contact address below.

Requested by others. The Help menu's Requested by others page lists feature and node requests the publisher has chosen to show, each described in the publisher's own words, never in the words of the person who asked; KeyPress fetches that list only when you open the page. Pressing +1 tells the support service to add one to that request's count, and nothing else: the service keeps the count and a one-way hash of a random code it gives back, so that you can take the vote back, and does not keep anything about you or your computer. Which requests you voted for is remembered only on your device. As with a report, Cloudflare sees your IP address to deliver the request, and the service uses it, without keeping it, to limit how quickly votes can be sent.

Your Choices and Controls

You can choose not to use recording, clipboard, screen-capture, OCR, or Anthropic-powered features. You can stop a recording, stop a macro, stop an AI authoring session, and configure the available recording and kill-switch controls.

You can delete saved macros, groups, local screenshots, and retained recording images from KeyPress or from your Documents\Keypress folder or the applicable local application-data location. You can remove a saved Anthropic API key through the application settings. Signing out in Settings removes the licence token and certificate from your device and asks the service to revoke the token; to stop being billed, cancel the subscription with Stripe, because signing out does not cancel it. To have the service’s record of your account deleted, write to the contact address below; deleting it also ends any licence or subscription it holds. To withdraw AI computer-use consent, remove the corresponding consent entries from KeyPress’s local configuration file; doing so prevents a new AI authoring session from starting until consent is given again.

Because macros may automate other software and services, deleting data in KeyPress does not delete any information that a macro has already entered into, copied to, saved in, or sent through those other services.

Security

KeyPress uses local storage and, on Windows, Windows user-scoped encryption for an API key, a licence token or a secret saved through the application. No system can guarantee absolute security. Protect your device account, application-data folders, and any exported or shared macro files, especially where they contain sensitive text, screenshots, or credentials.

Third-Party Services and Your Automations

This policy covers KeyPress, the KeyPress service and the KeyPress support service. Anthropic, Stripe, Cloudflare and GitHub each have their own privacy practices for what they receive. A macro may interact with websites, desktop applications, files, clipboards, or services you select. Those recipients and services have their own privacy practices, and KeyPress cannot control how they handle information once an automation supplies it to them.
